Consonants

There are 8 total handshapes used in Cued American English. Each handshape corresponds to multiple consonant sounds in the English language. Please take your time familiarizing yourself with the handshapes below and the sounds they represent.

As you practice each handshape, please position your hand a few inches away from your chest and your chin, as shown below. This form is used to represent consants without any vowel sounds (i.e. “mm” as opposed to “me” or “moo”), which will be discussed more in the next section.

Tip: Test yourself by voicing the sounds before flipping the panel after you’ve learned them.

Tip: Tip: Each symbol represents sound, not letters. Click each symbol or read the phrase to understand how it is voiced.
